4Three ways to plot effective price

Same 24 months of all-in €/kWh, three different questions. The plain line — the one 1b and 2a already use — answers “is it going up”. These answer why, is it real, and what has it cost. All three follow the 2a palette and respond to the theme switch.

4aSplit into what it is made of
Why is the price what it is?
Effective price is three things divided by kWh. Stacking them shows the standing charge behaving oddly — it is a fixed €5.90, so the fewer kWh you buy, the more each one carries.
ENERGY STANDING TAX ┊ JULY
The amber band swells every summer: {{ v4.a.fixedLow }}/kWh in a heavy January, {{ v4.a.fixedHigh }}/kWh in a light July. Cutting consumption raises your headline price per unit — worth knowing before reading that number as a tariff change.
4bAgainst how much you used
Is the rise real, or just lighter months?
One dot per bill — consumption across, price up. Time is in the colour: pale is two years ago, solid is last month. The curves are what a bill would cost at the old and new contract rates.
kWh USED → AT €0.2056 AT €0.1550
The dots have climbed off the lower curve onto the upper one at every consumption level, so the increase is not a usage artefact. The vertical gap between curves — {{ v4.b.gap }}/kWh — is the contract rise, clean of seasonality.
4cConverted back into money
What has the increase actually cost?
A running total of what each month cost above what it would have cost at the price you started on. Prices in €/kWh are abstract; this is the same series in euros you can count.

It runs below zero through the first winter — heavy months spread the standing charge thinner, so they beat the August baseline. The climb only becomes monotone once the contract rate overtakes that effect.
